== Reading

- With tiering on (the default), the typed handler drops to its steady cost only after 5 s
  (second pass) to 12 s (first pass) of load: the JIT's background thread shares the
  worker's vCPU, and tiered PGO recompiles twice (instrumented tier, then tier 1).
- Once converged, tiering on beats tiering off (593-708 against 783-1,048 in the second
  pass): PGO pays for the wait.
- With tiering off, the cost is flat from the second window: everything is compiled
  optimized at the first call, without PGO.
- The last pass (TieredPGO=0, second time) ran through heavy host interference: windows took
  2 s instead of 0.6 s, and the costs tripled. It shows the noise this host adds, not the JIT.
- Hence bench.sh's 7,000,000-call warm-up (about 10 s), and its tiering-off variant, which
  checks in every round that the default had converged.
